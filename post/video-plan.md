# Video plan (target 75 to 85 seconds)

Order follows post/demo-script.md and the post images (tools/gallery.py). The final shot is the
chapter in Google Docs with the reader's comments in the margin, never a number.

## Footage, and when to capture it

Most of it comes free from sessions that happen anyway, so no extra credits are spent on b-roll.

| Clip | What | Captured during | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Consent screen, Start pressed | graded read | Win+Alt+R (Game Bar) records the Unskipped window. Start it on the home screen |
| B | Reading: page, reading line, live rail, a flag appearing | graded read | same recording as A, keep it running to the end |
| C | Marks screen, then review, a flag clicked, one note dropped, Send | graded read | same recording |
| D | Looking at the phone: the paragraph gets clear film, "no reading" | planted-controls session (paragraph 2 or 8) | Win+Alt+R again |
| E | Writer screen: add the reading, "Write the notes into my manuscript" | Saturday | Win+Alt+R |
| F | The exported .docx opened in Google Docs, comments in the margin, scroll to a "drifted" comment | Saturday | screen recording of Chrome |
| G | Replay page on GitHub Pages, the headline number | Saturday, after graded.json is pushed | `py tools/gallery.py` stills, or a short recording |

Notes for F:
- Use your **personal** Google account in Chrome. The Google Drive connected to this session is the N2 one, and its address must not appear in the video. Check the avatar and the share dialog before recording.
- I checked on 19 Nov that Google Docs imports the comments the export writes: author name kept, each comment anchored on the right paragraph (placeholder file, then deleted).
- Upload the .docx to Drive and open it with Google Docs; the comments show in the margin without any setting.

Game Bar records only the active window and no desktop, which keeps notifications and other apps out of frame. Turn on Do Not Disturb before each session anyway.

## Shot list and narration

Read at an easy pace, about 150 words a minute. `[X]` values come from results/summary.json after the graded read (`npm run check` fills the post; copy the same numbers here).

| Time | Picture | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Text on cloth: three typical beta-reader replies ("Loved it!", "Great chapter!", "Really enjoyed this one.") | Every beta reader says they loved the chapter. None of them tells you where they stopped reading. |
| 0:07 | Elmore Leonard's line, cited (NYT, 16 July 2001) | Elmore Leonard said to leave out the part readers tend to skip. So which part is it? |
| 0:13 | A: home, then consent | This is Unskipped. The reader opens the chapter on their own laptop, and before the camera turns on, they see what it reads, that no video leaves the laptop, and that they approve every note. |
| 0:27 | B: reading, the rail live, a yellow then a pink flag | While they read, Presage SmartSpectra reads their pulse, blinks and smiles, and the paragraph under the reading line gets the credit. A smile leaves a yellow flag. Pulse up or blinking slowed leaves pink. Blinking speeds up, blue: they may have drifted. |
| 0:44 | D: reader looks at phone, clear film on that paragraph | If they look at their phone, that paragraph gets clear film. No reading. It says so instead of guessing. |
| 0:52 | C: marks screen | When they finish, they mark what got them and what lost them, before seeing a single flag. Those marks grade the camera. |
| 1:00 | C or G: review headline, fore-edge strip | I read a short story by Saki this way. Of the [X] paragraphs I marked as got me, the camera flagged [Y]. Time on the page alone found [Z]. |
| 1:10 | C: one note dropped, Send. E: writer screen, export | The reader keeps or drops each note, and sends the rest. |
| 1:15 | **F: Google Docs, comments in the margin, scroll to a drift comment** | The writer adds the reading, and every note lands as a comment on its paragraph, in their own manuscript. |

About 190 words, so 75 to 80 seconds spoken.

## Recording the narration

One take of the whole script, phone or laptop mic, quiet room. The demo-video skill cleans the audio and checks the raw take for clipping and echo before it cuts the clips to it.
